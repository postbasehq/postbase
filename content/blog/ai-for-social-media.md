**AI is good at the repetitive half of social media: turning one idea into versions for every network, repurposing videos and blog posts, writing captions and alt text, summarising what performed, and, with an AI agent, scheduling the result.** It's still bad at the half that makes accounts grow: original opinions, first-hand experience, accurate facts without a source, and replying to people like a person. The workflow that works is AI drafts, you decide, and nothing posts without your approval.

This guide is the hub for everything we've written about using AI for social media. It covers what's worth handing to AI, what isn't, the tools people actually use in 2026, what the platforms require you to label, and a weekly workflow you can copy. Written on 10 November 2026.

## What AI is good at on social media

**Adapting one idea for every network.** The same announcement needs to be 280 characters on X, 300 on Bluesky and a few short paragraphs on LinkedIn. This is where AI saves the most time, because the thinking is done and only the shape changes.

**Repurposing.** A YouTube video, podcast, blog post or changelog already contains a week of posts. AI can pull out the quotable lines, the numbers and the how-tos. Here's how we [turn one YouTube video into a week of posts](/blog/repurpose-youtube-video-into-social-posts).

**First drafts from notes.** Give it three bullet points and a tone, and it'll give you something to edit. Editing is faster than starting from a blank box.

**Captions, hooks and alt text.** Ten opening lines to choose from, a TikTok caption written for search, or a description of an image for people using screen readers.

**Summarising performance.** "Which of last month's posts did best, and what did they have in common?" is a good question for an AI that can read your analytics.

**Doing the busywork, as an agent.** Since late 2024, AI assistants like Claude and ChatGPT can use tools through MCP servers. Connected to a scheduler, they can check what's already queued, find a free slot and schedule a post, all from the chat. If MCP is new to you, start with [what an MCP server is](/blog/what-is-an-mcp-server).

## What AI still gets wrong

**Opinions and experience.** The posts that build an audience say something only you could say: what you learned, what you disagree with, what happened to you. AI can polish that, not invent it. Posts written entirely by AI tend to sound like every other AI post, and readers notice.

**Facts.** AI writes confident sentences about features, prices and dates that are wrong. Give it the source (the changelog, the doc, the transcript) and check anything specific before it goes out.

**Timing and context.** It doesn't know that today is a bad day to post a joke, or that your customers are having an outage. A human has to look at the calendar before things go out.

**Conversation.** Replies and comments are where relationships form. Automated replies feel automated. Use AI to draft a tricky reply if you like, but send it yourself.

**Images of real things.** Generated images of real people, products or events can mislead, and the platforms increasingly require you to label them (see below).

## The kinds of AI tools for social media

There are four broad kinds, and most people end up using two of them together.

**1. General AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini).** Best for writing and thinking. On their own they produce text you copy into each app. Connected to a scheduler through an MCP server, they can also schedule. Guides: [post to social media from Claude](/blog/post-to-social-media-from-claude) and [write LinkedIn posts with Claude](/blog/write-linkedin-posts-with-claude).

**2. AI built into schedulers.** Most scheduling tools now have an assistant in the composer that rewrites, shortens or suggests captions. Convenient, because the text is already where it'll be posted.

**3. AI agents and automations.** Agents that run a whole job: research topics, draft posts, have them reviewed, schedule them. You can build these with Claude Code ([a content team with agent teams](/blog/claude-code-agent-teams)), with automation tools like n8n ([automate social media with n8n](/blog/automate-social-media-with-n8n)), or use one built into your scheduler.

**4. Image and video generators.** For thumbnails, illustrations and B-roll. Useful, but the area where platform labelling rules apply most.

## AI labelling rules on each platform

Using AI to write a caption doesn't need a label anywhere. Realistic AI-generated or altered images, video and audio increasingly do:

- **YouTube** requires creators to disclose content that's [realistic and altered or synthetic](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), such as making a real person appear to say something they didn't or generating a realistic scene that didn't happen. Using AI for scripts, titles, thumbnails, captions or ideas doesn't need disclosure.
- **TikTok** requires a label on AI-generated content that shows realistic scenes, people or audio, and can label some content automatically, for example when it carries Content Credentials.
- **LinkedIn** shows a Content Credentials icon on images and videos that carry that metadata, so viewers can see they were made or edited with AI.
- **Meta (Instagram, Facebook, Threads)** adds "AI info" labels to content it detects or that creators mark as AI-generated.
- **X** has a policy against synthetic or manipulated media that's likely to mislead, and may label or remove it.

The safe rule: if a reasonable viewer could mistake AI-made media for something real, label it.

## A weekly AI workflow that keeps you in control

1. **Collect material (10 minutes).** Notes, links, a changelog, a video transcript, last week's best posts.
2. **Ask for drafts.** "Turn these into three LinkedIn posts, five X posts and five Bluesky posts for next week. Keep my voice: short sentences, no hashtags, no emojis."
3. **Edit like an editor.** Add the opinion or example only you have. Cut anything you wouldn't say out loud. Check every fact.
4. **Schedule into good times.** Ask the agent to spread them across the week at your best hours, or do it yourself on a calendar. Our [best time to post tool](/tools/best-time-to-post) gives starting times for each network.
5. **Approve before anything goes out.** Whether you schedule them or an agent does, nothing should publish that a person hasn't read.
6. **Review once a week.** Ask the AI what did best and why, then decide what to do more of.

The approval step matters most once an agent can post for you. Our guide to [letting an AI agent post safely](/blog/let-an-ai-agent-post-safely) covers the guardrails: limited tools, approval before publishing, and being able to cut off access in one click.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can AI be used for social media?

To draft posts from notes, adapt one post for several networks, repurpose videos and articles, write captions and alt text, summarise analytics, and, with an AI agent connected to a scheduler, schedule the posts. Keep a person in charge of opinions, facts and replies.

### What is the best AI for social media content creation?

For writing, general assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT are the strongest, especially when you give them your notes and examples of your voice. The best setup connects one of them to your scheduler so drafts become scheduled posts without copying and pasting.

### Can AI post to social media for me?

Yes. AI agents connected to a scheduler through an MCP server or API can schedule and publish posts. The safest setups ask for your approval first and let you revoke access at any time.

### Do I have to label AI-generated posts?

Not for text written with AI help. Realistic AI-generated or altered images, video and audio need a label on YouTube and TikTok, and other platforms add labels automatically when they detect AI content.

### Will AI-written posts hurt my reach?

The platforms don't penalise text for being written with AI. What hurts reach is generic content: posts that sound like everyone else's get less engagement. Edit AI drafts until they sound like you.

### Is it worth using AI for social media as a small business?

Usually, yes, for drafting and repurposing, which are the most time-consuming parts. Spend the time it saves on replying to people and making the few posts that need your own voice.
