## When is Postbase the best alternative to Buffer?

Shortlist Postbase when your publishing starts in an AI conversation and you want to compare bundled channel allowances with Buffer's per-channel bill. The [Postbase homepage](https://www.postbase.so/) describes an in-app agent, MCP connections for external AI tools, a REST API and an open-source version you can run yourself.

The [pricing page](https://www.postbase.so/pricing) lists Creator at **$29/month for 5 channels, 1 person and 1 workspace**, and Team at **$39/month for 15 channels, 5 people and 3 workspaces**. Both include MCP and API access. That's a useful shortlist for a founder or small team publishing across several accounts.

Check the limitations first. The [homepage's network FAQ](https://www.postbase.so/#faq) lists X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Mastodon, TikTok and YouTube; it marks Instagram, Facebook and Threads as coming soon and says TikTok publishing is private for now. If public TikTok campaigns or Instagram publishing are essential today, choose a tool that meets those requirements now.

The [plan terms](https://www.postbase.so/pricing) also limit X posts containing links: **20/month on Creator and 30/month on Team**. The seven-day cloud trial requires a card, with smaller AI and X-link allowances until payment. Cancel before it ends to avoid a charge.

Buffer also has an [API and MCP server](https://buffer.com/api). Postbase's case is the combination of workflow, hosting choice and plan capacity, not exclusive access to AI automation. See the [Postbase and Buffer comparison](/alternatives/buffer) for more context.

## Which alternatives are better for specific needs?

### Metricool: reporting and competitor analysis

Metricool is our first shortlist when reporting is the reason to leave Buffer. Its Free plan includes one brand, 20 scheduled posts per month, five competitor profiles and 30 days of analytics. It excludes LinkedIn and X. Starter begins at $25/month for up to five brands and adds PDF/PPT reports; X is an add-on. Team management and approvals sit in Advanced. [Metricool plans](https://metricool.com/pricing/).

Test a sample report with the metrics your client actually requests. A dashboard full of numbers is less useful than a report you can explain and deliver every month. A brand allowance also isn't interchangeable with an arbitrary number of connected accounts.

### Publer: a free queue for a few accounts

Publer is worth trying if you want to compare everyday scheduling without paying immediately. Free includes three accounts excluding X, with ten scheduled posts per account. Those slots become available again after posts publish or are deleted; this is a queue limit, not a monthly publishing quota. [Publer plans](https://publer.com/plans).

Paid pricing depends on accounts and additional members. Professional adds RSS automation; Business adds analytics and API/MCP access. Price the tier you actually need rather than assuming the entry plan includes every automation feature. If Buffer's free queue already fits, test whether Publer improves your weekly routine enough to justify moving.

### Later: visual planning for Instagram-focused brands

Later is a strong shortlist for a team planning visual content across Instagram and other supported networks. Starter is advertised at **$18.75/month billed yearly**, with one user, one social set and 30 scheduled posts per profile per month. A social set contains one profile from each supported network; it doesn't mean eight interchangeable Instagram accounts. [Later pricing and limits](https://later.com/pricing/).

Growth adds collaboration and approvals. Before switching, map your accounts to social sets and test the actual Reels, Stories, carousels and other formats you publish. Starter's post allowance is monthly, unlike a free queue whose slots refill.

### SocialPilot: agencies needing client approval and reporting

Evaluate SocialPilot Premium if your team needs client approval, bulk scheduling and white-label reports. The published monthly price is **$100 for 25 accounts and 6 users**. Standard covers 15 accounts and 3 users with manager approval, while Premium adds client approval. That distinction matters when an external client must sign off. [SocialPilot plans](https://www.socialpilot.co/plans).

Run a trial with a reviewer using the access they would have in practice. Check feedback, revisions and the final report before deciding that a larger account allowance solves your agency's problem.

### Planable: approvals as the center of the workflow

Planable is our shortlist when the main problem is getting content reviewed. Basic has optional approval; Pro adds required approval; Enterprise supports multiple approval stages. Pick the workflow before the price. [Planable approval tiers](https://planable.io/pricing/).

Pro costs **$59 per workspace per month** on monthly billing. Analytics costs another $14/workspace/month and Social Inbox $9/workspace/month. Its trial allows 50 created posts in total, not a recurring monthly allowance. [Planable billing documentation](https://help.planable.io/hc/en-us/articles/21715370520092-Questions-on-pricing).

For example, three Pro workspaces with Analytics cost **3 × ($59 + $14) = $219/month**, before applicable tax. That's our calculation from the published rates. Budget for each client's workspace, then test whether the review process earns that cost back in your team's time.

### Postiz: another route to self-hosting

Postiz deserves a place beside Postbase when hosting control matters. Its [Docker Compose guide](https://docs.postiz.com/self-host/installation/docker-compose) documents running your own installation. Its cloud Standard plan is $29/month for five channels, with MCP, CLI and API access. Its published network list includes Instagram, Facebook, Threads, Pinterest, Reddit and Discord, alongside other networks. [Postiz cloud plans](https://postiz.com/pricing).

Treat cloud and self-hosting as separate purchasing decisions. For self-hosting, budget for infrastructure, storage, provider credentials, updates and recovery. Assign an owner before moving a business publishing calendar. The [Postiz vs Buffer guide](/blog/postiz-vs-buffer) explores that decision in more detail.

### SocialBee: categories and evergreen content

SocialBee is worth a trial if you repeatedly publish a mix of tutorials, product explainers and other reusable material. It supports content categories, recycling and expiration rules. Bootstrap costs **$29/month for five profiles**, with one user, one workspace and ten content categories. [SocialBee pricing and features](https://socialbee.com/pricing/).

Start with a small library and check how each category feeds the schedule. Give time-sensitive posts an expiration date, and review recycled posts when product details or offers change. The value here is whether maintaining that library makes your routine easier.

## Is an alternative actually cheaper than Buffer?

Compare the same billing period and enough capacity for your real workload. Here's a **monthly-billing subscription comparison in USD**, using Buffer's published volume bands and Postbase's listed plans:

| Connected accounts | Buffer Essentials | Buffer Team | Postbase plan with enough channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | $18/month | $36/month | Creator: $29/month |
| 5 | $30/month | $60/month | Creator: $29/month |
| 15 | $80/month | $140/month | Team: $39/month |

Buffer's first ten channels cost $6 each on Essentials or $12 each on Team; channels 11–25 cost $4 each on either plan. Thus, 15-channel Essentials is **10 × $6 + 5 × $4 = $80**, not $90. Sources: [Buffer pricing bands](https://support.buffer.com/articles/buffer-pricing-and-features-6pJrOPuzIt), [Postbase plans](https://www.postbase.so/pricing).

These compare channel capacity, not equivalent features. Buffer Essentials is for one user; Team includes unlimited users and approvals. Postbase's prices include applicable sales tax or VAT; compare final checkout totals for your location. Exclude any option that cannot handle your formats, team or posting volume before choosing by price.

A useful budget is **subscription + add-ons + migration time + ongoing administration**. For self-hosting, also include infrastructure and maintenance. A lower subscription price is only one part of the decision.

## When should you keep Buffer?

Keep Buffer if it already covers your networks and your workflow. Its Free plan supports three channels with ten queued posts per channel, with slots refilling after publication. It also includes API access, so needing an integration alone is not a reason to leave. [Buffer's current Free plan](https://buffer.com/pricing).

If your frustration is approvals, check Team before migrating: Buffer already offers that feature. If it is automation, try its [GraphQL API or MCP server](https://buffer.com/api). Test whether an alternative solves a specific problem rather than moving because an old comparison says Buffer lacks something it now supports.

## How do you switch without publishing twice?

1. **Inventory your workflow.** List accounts, account types, post formats, reviewers, time zones and reporting requirements.
2. **Test a representative week.** Include a video, a thread or carousel if relevant, and any client review step. Verify the published result on each network.
3. **Record your existing queue.** Keep the text, media, links and intended times. Save any reports or history you need while you still have access.
4. **Move posts in small batches.** Verify each replacement, then remove its original scheduled copy before either publish time. Don't leave the same post active in two schedulers.
5. **Complete the handover.** Check failures and notifications, confirm the team can operate the new tool, and cancel the old subscription only after the queue is reconciled.

Don't assume an importer moves analytics, drafts or approval history. Check those items separately during the trial.
