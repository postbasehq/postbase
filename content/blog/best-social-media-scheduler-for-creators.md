The best social media scheduler for creators depends on what slows you down: choose **Postbase** for drafting and scheduling through AI assistants, **Buffer** for a free queue across a few accounts, or **Later** for visual Instagram planning. Choose **Metricool** when reporting drives your decisions, and **Typefully** when writing threads is the main job. Postbase fits creators on X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Mastodon and YouTube; Instagram, Facebook and Threads are still coming, and its TikTok publishing is private-only for now ([current networks](https://www.postbase.so/#faq)).

We make Postbase. This is a comparison of published features and limits, not a hands-on speed or reliability test. We checked the linked vendor pages on October 31, 2026; the recommendations are our judgment about which workflow each product suits.

## When is Postbase the best fit for a creator?

Pick Postbase when you already develop ideas in Claude or ChatGPT and want to turn those conversations into scheduled posts. Its [hosted MCP server](https://www.postbase.so/mcp) connects those assistants to drafting and scheduling, with posts visible on a calendar. MCP is the connection between the assistant and the scheduler; you don't need to write code to use it.

For example, ask an assistant to turn a tutorial into an X thread and a LinkedIn post, review both drafts, then choose when to publish. That is a suggested workflow, not a claim that we measured time savings. It is especially useful when the source material already lives in your AI conversation.

In the [Postbase dashboard](https://www.postbase.so/), you can also customize a draft for each network, review a month, week or day, and use the built-in agent. Its proposed posts need your confirmation before scheduling.

The [Creator plan](https://www.postbase.so/pricing) costs $29/month for five channels, one person and one workspace, including calendar, drafts, analytics, MCP and API access. It includes 20 X posts containing links per month: count newsletter and affiliate promotions before choosing a plan. The seven-day trial requires a card and converts to paid billing unless canceled; trial allowances are smaller.

**Choose another tool if Instagram, Facebook or Threads is essential today.** For public TikTok campaigns, Postbase is also not ready: its [TikTok integration page](https://www.postbase.so/integrations/tiktok) says posts remain private pending approval. A network logo alone is not enough to establish that your publishing workflow is supported.

## When should you choose Buffer instead?

Buffer is our starting pick for a solo creator who wants to try scheduling without a subscription. Its [Free plan](https://buffer.com/pricing) supports three channels and ten queued posts per channel. That is a queue capacity, not ten posts per month: a published post frees up a slot.

For a creator posting daily, a ten-slot queue can cover a week on each account, but it cannot hold a whole month at once. The free plan also lists an AI assistant and API access, so paying for Postbase is a workflow choice, not a requirement for using AI with a scheduler.

[Essentials](https://buffer.com/pricing.md) starts at $6 per channel per month on monthly billing, or $5 with annual billing. At five channels, monthly Essentials is $30 versus Postbase Creator's $29. That $1 difference should not decide the purchase: supported formats and how you like to write matter more. Buffer's annual option is $25/month for five channels, billed yearly, so keep billing periods consistent when comparing.

## Is Later better for Instagram and short-form video?

Later is our pick when the arrangement of your Instagram feed is part of the creative process. Its [visual planner](https://later.com/social-media-scheduler/) lets you preview and rearrange the feed, and its calendar brings planning for several networks together. That makes it worth trying for photography, fashion, food and other visual portfolios.

[Starter](https://later.com/pricing/) includes one user and one social set: one profile for each of eight supported networks. The quoted $18.75/month is billed annually, and the plan allows 30 posts per profile per month. Two posts a day on one profile would exceed that allowance; having unused capacity on another profile does not change that per-profile cap.

Its current network list includes Instagram, Facebook, Threads, Pinterest, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube and Snapchat. X, Bluesky and Mastodon are absent from that list. Later offers a 14-day trial; don't treat a trial as a permanent free plan.

## When is Metricool worth choosing?

Choose Metricool if your weekly routine includes reviewing results or preparing sponsor reports. Its [pricing page](https://metricool.com/pricing/) lists PDF and PowerPoint reports and unlimited analytics history on Starter, which starts at $25/month or $20/month with annual billing.

Free covers one brand, 20 scheduled posts per month and 30 days of analytics. It excludes LinkedIn and X; paid plans offer LinkedIn, while X requires an add-on. The monthly post allowance makes it a smaller free publishing budget than a refillable queue for someone posting daily. Compare the reporting you actually need before upgrading: a useful report answers a decision, such as which series to repeat or which channel deserves more production time.

## What if you mostly write threads and LinkedIn posts?

Try Typefully if writing and revising text is the work you want a scheduler to improve. Its [product page](https://typefully.com/) describes a focused editor, thread previews, shared drafts and cross-posting to X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Threads and Mastodon. It also advertises API and MCP access, so it belongs on an AI-assisted creator's shortlist too.

It offers a free plan. Check [current paid terms](https://typefully.com/pricing) before upgrading; we have not quoted a paid price because the public pricing page did not expose its plan details during this review. For a workflow that also requires Reels, TikTok or YouTube, verify coverage before making it your only scheduler.

## How should you test a scheduler before paying?

Use a real week of content. A trial full of placeholder posts tells you little about whether the tool handles your work.

1. **Write down the exact accounts and formats.** Include carousels, threads, video, first comments, sponsored disclosures and any audio you need. Ask whether each format publishes automatically or requires a reminder and a manual step.
2. **Prepare one representative batch.** Use an actual video, a text post and a campaign link. Check previews, captions and destination accounts before scheduling.
3. **Change something after scheduling.** Move a post, edit a caption and cancel a draft you no longer want. Those everyday corrections matter as much as the initial upload.
4. **Check the result on the network.** Confirm visibility, media, links and timing. A successful upload is not necessarily a publicly visible post.
5. **Price your full month.** Count accounts, collaborators, scheduled posts and add-ons. For sponsored work, include any approval process you need before publishing.

Keep the trial small enough to review every post. Our [content calendar guide](/blog/social-media-content-calendar-template) can help you map the batch before moving it into a tool.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free social media scheduler for creators?

Buffer is our first pick for a free queue across a few accounts. Its [Free plan](https://buffer.com/pricing) allows three channels with ten queued posts per channel, refillable as posts publish. Check your exact formats before moving your schedule.

### Which scheduler works with Claude or ChatGPT?

Postbase's [MCP server](https://www.postbase.so/mcp) lets compatible assistants draft and schedule posts, with a calendar for checking the result. Typefully also advertises MCP access on its [product page](https://typefully.com/). Compare the actual drafting and review flow, not just whether a tool says it has AI.

### Can Postbase publish Instagram Reels or public TikTok videos?

Not currently. [Instagram is coming soon](https://www.postbase.so/#faq), and Postbase's [TikTok posts are private-only](https://www.postbase.so/integrations/tiktok) pending approval. Choose a tool that supports your required public format today rather than planning a campaign around a future integration.

### Is a free plan enough for a full month of content?

It depends on how limits are counted. A queue limit measures posts waiting to publish; a monthly allowance measures usage during that month. Work out whether you need to load the entire month at once or can refill the queue weekly, then check the vendor's counting rules for cross-posts and threads.

### Should a solo creator self-host a scheduler?

Only if running the software is an acceptable part of the job. Postbase offers a [free self-hosted option](https://www.postbase.so/pricing) using your own platform API keys, but you still need to budget for infrastructure and maintenance. A managed plan is usually the more practical starting point when your time belongs in creating and publishing.
