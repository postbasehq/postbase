**The best time to post on LinkedIn in 2026 is Tuesday to Thursday between 10am and 5pm in your audience's local time, and the best day is Wednesday.** Sprout Social's study of 2 billion engagements puts the peak between 11am and 5pm on weekdays; Buffer's study of 4.8 million posts found Wednesday at 4pm the single best slot. Weekends are the weakest days for almost everyone.

If you've read that early mornings are best on LinkedIn, that was the old advice. The two largest 2026 datasets both moved the peak to late morning and afternoon. Here's the detail, checked on 10 November 2026. For every network in one table, see the [best time to post on social media](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-social-media).

Times are in your audience's local time. Sources: [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-linkedin/) and [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/).

Where they overlap, **Tuesday to Thursday, about 3pm to 5pm**, is the safest slot of the week.

## The best day to post on LinkedIn

**Wednesday**, with Tuesday and Thursday close behind. Buffer ranks Wednesday first, then Thursday, then Friday, and calls Monday the weakest weekday. Sprout rates every weekday well but gives Tuesday to Thursday the longest high-engagement windows.

Mondays are busy with catch-up and meetings, and Fridays wind down early, which leaves the middle of the week. If you post three times a week, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday is a reliable rhythm.

## Why afternoons now beat early mornings

Older studies, and many guides still repeating them, put LinkedIn's peak at 7am to 9am, when people checked the app before work. Hootsuite's 2025 data still leaned that way. The 2026 data from Sprout and Buffer points later, to late morning and mid-afternoon.

A likely reason is how LinkedIn's feed works now: posts are shown for days, not hours, and are ranked largely on how people engage with them rather than how recent they are. A post that goes out at 8am competes with everyone's morning batch; one that goes out mid-afternoon lands when people take a break between meetings and have time to read and comment. Whatever the reason, the newest and largest datasets agree, so test afternoons first.

## How to find your own best time on LinkedIn

The studies average hundreds of thousands of accounts. Your audience may be in a single time zone, on shifts, or senior people who read LinkedIn in the evening.

1. **Check where your followers are.** In your profile's analytics, look at your followers' locations. Post for the time zone most of them are in.
2. **Pick two slots to test**, for example Tuesday at 11am and Wednesday at 4pm.
3. **Post similar content in each** for three or four weeks.
4. **Compare impressions and comments** after a few days, since LinkedIn posts keep picking up views for two or three days.
5. **Keep the winner**, then test it against another slot.

## More than timing

Timing gives a post a fair start. These matter more:

- **The first two lines.** LinkedIn cuts posts off after a few lines with "...more". If those lines don't earn the click, nothing below them is read.
- **Comments, early.** Reply to every comment in the first hour or two. Conversations keep a post in front of people.
- **Links.** Many people put links in the first comment rather than the post. Our guide to the [link in the first comment](/blog/linkedin-link-in-first-comment) covers whether it's worth it.
- **Frequency.** Two to five posts a week is plenty for most people; more than one a day mostly competes with itself. More in [how often to post on social media](/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to post on LinkedIn?

Tuesday to Thursday between 10am and 5pm in your audience's local time. Sprout Social's 2026 data peaks from 11am to 5pm on weekdays, and Buffer's found Wednesday at 4pm the single best slot.

### What is the best day to post on LinkedIn?

Wednesday, followed by Tuesday and Thursday. Weekends are the weakest days, and Monday was the weakest weekday in Buffer's 2026 data.

### Is it better to post on LinkedIn in the morning or afternoon?

Afternoon, according to the 2026 studies. Late morning works too, especially for finance and tech audiences, but the old advice to post at 7am to 9am no longer matches the data.

### Should I post on LinkedIn on weekends?

Usually not. Engagement drops sharply on Saturday and Sunday in both studies. Post during the week and let weekend browsers find the posts later.

### What is the best time to post on LinkedIn in EST?

Tuesday to Thursday, 1pm to 4pm Eastern. For a national US audience, 2pm to 3pm Eastern lands at 11am to 12pm on the West Coast.

### How often should I post on LinkedIn?

Two to five times a week for most people. LinkedIn posts keep getting views for two or three days, so posting more than once a day mostly competes with your own posts.

### Does posting time matter on LinkedIn?

Yes, but less than the post itself. LinkedIn shows posts for days, so a strong post at an average time still does well. Timing helps by putting it in front of people when they have time to read and comment.
