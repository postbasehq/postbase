**The best time to post on TikTok in 2026 is Tuesday to Thursday between 2pm and 6pm in your audience's local time, according to Sprout Social's study of nearly 2 billion engagements.** Creator accounts often do better in the evening and at weekends: Buffer's study of 7.1 million posts found Saturday the strongest day. After a few weeks of posting, use your own follower activity in TikTok Studio instead.

That's the short answer. The longer one is that the big studies disagree, and the reasons they disagree tell you which one to follow. This guide puts their numbers side by side, gives a day-by-day table, converts the times for US audiences, and shows how to find the hours that work for your own account. Everything was checked on 10 November 2026. For every network in one table, see the [best time to post on social media](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-social-media).

## Why the studies disagree

They measure different accounts and different things.

**Sprout Social** looks at its own customers: mostly brands, agencies and organisations. It counts total engagements. Brands post on weekdays, and their audiences engage with them in the working week, so weekdays win and weekends look empty. If you run a company account, especially B2B, this is the closest match.

**Buffer** looks at a wider mix, including many creators and small businesses, and ranks slots by **median engagement rate**: how well a typical post does relative to how many people saw it. A likely reason weekends score well: fewer accounts post then, so each post faces less competition. If you're a creator or sell to consumers, Buffer's evenings and weekends are worth testing.

**Hootsuite** sits between the two, and its data is a year older.

So the rule of thumb is: **businesses start with weekday afternoons, creators start with evenings and Saturdays, and everyone moves to their own data as soon as they have it.**

## Best time to post on TikTok in EST, CST and PST

All three studies give times in your audience's local time, so 2pm means 2pm wherever your viewers are. The catch is a national audience spread across time zones. If most of your followers are in the US, aim at Eastern time: it's the most populous zone, and a 2pm Eastern post still lands at lunchtime on the West Coast.

| Sprout's window, Eastern | Central | Mountain | Pacific |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2pm ET | 1pm CT | 12pm MT | 11am PT |
| 4pm ET | 3pm CT | 2pm MT | 1pm PT |
| 6pm ET | 5pm CT | 4pm MT | 3pm PT |

If your audience is mostly on the West Coast, flip it and post for Pacific time. If it's split between the US and Europe, there's no single slot that suits both; post at different times on different days and compare.

## How to find your own best time on TikTok

Studies average hundreds of thousands of accounts. Your audience may be students in one country, night-shift workers, or parents who scroll after bedtime. TikTok shows you when your own followers are on the app:

1. Open **TikTok Studio** (on the web, or from your profile in the app).
2. Go to **Analytics**, then the **Followers** tab.
3. Find **Follower activity**, which shows the hours and days your followers are most active.
4. Note the **time zone** shown next to the chart. Depending on the version, it may be UTC rather than your local time, so convert before you schedule.

TikTok only fills this in once your account has some followers, and it needs a Creator or Business account. Then run a simple test:

1. **Pick three slots**: one from the studies above, your follower peak from TikTok Studio, and one an hour or two before that peak.
2. **Post similar videos** in each slot for two to three weeks. Change the time, not the type of content.
3. **Compare views and watch time** at 24 hours, not total views weeks later. The first day is where timing makes a difference.
4. **Keep the winner**, then test it against a new slot next month.

Many creators post an hour or two before their follower peak, so a video has picked up its first views and is being shown more widely by the time the most people are scrolling. That's a heuristic, not a rule, which is why it's one of the slots in the test.

## Does posting time matter on TikTok?

Less than on X or Bluesky, but yes. TikTok's For You feed isn't in time order: it shows each new video to a small group of viewers and decides from how they respond whether to show it to more. A video posted while your followers are asleep gets a slower first hour.

But timing is a small lever next to the video itself. The first second, how many people watch to the end, and whether they share or rewatch matter far more. A strong video posted at a quiet time can still take off days later; a weak one at the perfect minute won't. Use timing to give good videos a fair start, and spend most of your effort on the videos.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to post on TikTok?

Tuesday to Thursday between 2pm and 6pm in your audience's local time is the best starting point, based on Sprout Social's 2026 study of nearly 2 billion engagements. Creator accounts often do well in the evening and on Saturdays, according to Buffer's study of 7.1 million posts.

### What is the best day to post on TikTok?

For business accounts, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. For creators, Saturday ranked highest in Buffer's 2026 data. Sunday is the weakest day in Sprout Social's data, so it's the day to test last.

### Is it better to post on TikTok in the morning or at night?

Afternoon and early evening are the safest. Brand accounts peak between 2pm and 6pm; creator accounts often do well from 6pm to 10pm. Early mornings work on some days but vary a lot between studies.

### What time is TikTok most active?

Most people use TikTok in the afternoon and evening, after school and work. Your own followers' most active hours are in TikTok Studio, under Analytics, then Followers.

### Should I post on TikTok at the same time every day?

It helps to be consistent, because your followers learn when to expect you. But test a few slots first, and keep the one that gets the most views in the first 24 hours.

### How many times a day should you post on TikTok?

Three videos a week is a good start, and one a day if you can keep the quality up. Buffer's study of 11.4 million TikTok posts found that two to five posts a week gave about 17% more views per post than one a week, with smaller gains after that. There's more in [how often to post on social media](/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media).

### Does it matter what time zone I post in?

Post for your audience's time zone, not yours. If you're in London and your followers are in New York, 3pm Eastern is 8pm for you. Our [best time to post tool](/tools/best-time-to-post) converts the times for you.

### Does posting time affect the TikTok algorithm?

Only indirectly. TikTok tests each video on a small audience first, so posting when your followers are active gives it a better first hour. The video's watch time and shares matter far more than the minute it went out.
