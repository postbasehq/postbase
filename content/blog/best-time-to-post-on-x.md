**The best time to post on X (Twitter) in 2026 is Tuesday to Thursday, between about 9am and 1pm in your audience's local time, and the best day is Wednesday.** Buffer's study of 8.7 million posts found Tuesday at 9am the single best slot, with mornings strongest every day. Sprout Social's brand data puts the peak later, from 12pm to 6pm. Weekends are the weakest days in both.

The overlap, late morning to lunchtime midweek, is the safest place to start. Here's the detail, checked on 10 November 2026.

Buffer's morning pattern is remarkably steady: 9am to 11am wins on every day of the week. Sprout's later window reflects brand accounts, whose followers engage with them through the working afternoon.

## Why the two studies differ

They measure different accounts and different things. Buffer's data covers a broad mix of creators and small businesses and ranks by **engagement rate**, how well a post does relative to its views. Early posts catch people checking X before work and face less competition. Sprout's data comes from brand and organisation accounts and counts **total engagement**, which peaks when the most people are online, in the afternoon.

Practical takeaway:

- **Creators and individuals:** start with 9am to 11am.
- **Brands and B2B accounts:** start with 12pm to 3pm.
- **Everyone:** Tuesday to Thursday, and test the other window against yours.

## Why timing matters more on X than most networks

Posts on X have a short life. Most of a post's views come in the first hours, as followers see it in their timelines and replies push it further. A post that goes out while your audience is asleep gets fewer of those early replies and reposts, and it's quickly buried.

That's also why **replying quickly** matters: answering the first replies in the first 30 to 60 minutes keeps the conversation, and the post, in front of people.

## How to find your own best time on X

1. If you have **X Premium**, open your analytics dashboard (x.com/i/account_analytics) and check when your followers are active.
2. Without Premium, compare your own posts: note the time each went out and its impressions and engagements from **View post analytics**. Our guide to [X analytics](/blog/x-twitter-analytics) shows where.
3. **Test two windows** for three weeks, for example 9am and 1pm, with similar posts.
4. **Compare engagement rate**, not raw impressions, and keep the winner.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to post on X (Twitter)?

Tuesday to Thursday between about 9am and 1pm in your audience's local time. Buffer's 2026 study found Tuesday at 9am the best single slot; Sprout Social's brand data peaks from 12pm to 6pm.

### What is the best day to post on X?

Wednesday, followed by Tuesday. Saturday is the weakest day, and Friday is the weakest weekday in Buffer's data.

### Is it better to tweet in the morning or afternoon?

Mornings, from 9am to 11am, did best in Buffer's 2026 data across every day of the week. Brand accounts in Sprout Social's data peaked in the afternoon. Test both against your own audience.

### What is the best time to tweet in EST?

12pm Eastern works best for a national US audience. If your audience is mostly on the East Coast, try 9am to 11am Eastern.

### Should I post on X on weekends?

Weekends are the weakest days in both studies. Saturday was the lowest in Buffer's data; Sunday morning is the better of the two if you post at weekends.

### Does posting time matter on X?

More than on most networks, because posts get most of their views in the first few hours. Posting when your followers are online, and replying quickly, gives each post a better start.
