There's no magic number, but there are sensible ranges. Post too rarely and nobody remembers you; post too often and each post does worse, and you run out of things to say. The goal is the most posts you can keep up, at a quality you're proud of, for months.

This guide gives a starting range for each network, what the research behind it says, and a plan for keeping the rhythm going. It was written on 25 October 2026.

## The short version

| Network | Starting point | If you have more to say | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| X | 1 post a day | 3 to 5 a day, plus replies | Replies count as much as posts |
| LinkedIn | 2 to 3 posts a week | Up to 5 a week | More than one a day competes with itself |
| Bluesky | 3 to 5 posts a week | 1 to 3 a day | Chronological feeds reward steady posting |
| Mastodon | 3 to 5 posts a week | 1 to 3 a day, spread out | Never post in bursts |
| TikTok | 3 videos a week | 1 a day | Consistency matters more than volume |
| YouTube (long videos) | 1 a week, or 2 a month | 2 a week | Quality beats frequency |
| YouTube Shorts | 3 a week | 1 a day | Treat like TikTok |

If you're starting from nothing, pick the left-hand column for one or two networks and stick to it for eight weeks before changing anything.

## What the research says

Most posting-frequency research comes from scheduling tools analysing their own customers' posts, so it's a guide rather than a law. The most consistent findings:

- **More posts usually means more total reach, but less reach per post.** Each extra post adds a bit less than the one before.
- **Consistency beats bursts.** Accounts that post steadily for months grow faster than accounts that post a lot for two weeks and then stop.
- **On LinkedIn, two to five posts a week is the sweet spot.** Buffer's analysis of more than two million LinkedIn posts found that range gave the best balance of reach and engagement, which matches LinkedIn's own advice to post at least a few times a week.
- **On short-video platforms, regular uploads matter.** TikTok and YouTube Shorts test each video with a small audience first, so more videos means more chances for one to travel.

## Network by network

### X

X moves fast, and a post is mostly gone from timelines within a few hours. One post a day is a good minimum; accounts that grow quickly often post three to five times a day, plus a lot of replies to others. Replies are underrated: thoughtful replies to bigger accounts in your niche put you in front of new people with no extra content to write.

A thread a week is a good way to share something longer. Here's [how to write one people finish](/blog/how-to-write-an-x-thread).

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn posts have a longer life. A good post can keep picking up views for two or three days, so posting more than once a day mostly makes your own posts compete. Two to three posts a week is plenty for most people, and five is the ceiling for nearly everyone.

Comments matter more than volume here. Ten minutes a day commenting on other people's posts often does more than an extra post.

### Bluesky

Bluesky's default Following feed is chronological, and popular custom feeds favour recent posts. That makes steady posting more important than on LinkedIn. Three to five posts a week keeps you visible; active accounts post once to three times a day. See our guide to [growing on Bluesky](/blog/how-to-grow-on-bluesky).

### Mastodon

Mastodon has no algorithm, so posts appear in followers' timelines in the order they arrive. Several posts in a row fill people's timelines and feel like spam. Spread your posts across the day, and keep to one to three a day at most.

### TikTok

Three videos a week is a sensible starting point, and one a day if you can keep the quality. TikTok shows each new video to a small test audience first, so frequent posting gives you more chances to break out. Don't delete videos that flop; just post the next one.

### YouTube

For long videos, quality and consistency matter far more than frequency. One video a week, or every other week, on a schedule your viewers can rely on, is better than three rushed ones. Shorts behave more like TikTok: three a week is a good start, and the same vertical video can go to both. ## Signs you're posting too much (or too little)

**Too much:**

- Average engagement per post keeps falling, week after week.
- You're posting things you wouldn't stop to read yourself.
- Followers are dropping after busy days.
- You dread writing.

**Too little:**

- Every post feels like it starts from zero.
- Followers forget who you are between posts.
- You have plenty to say, but it sits in drafts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many times a day should you post on social media?

It depends on the network. On X, once a day is a good minimum and three to five is common for growing accounts. On LinkedIn, aim for two to five posts a week, not per day. On Bluesky and Mastodon, one to three a day spread out. On TikTok, three a week to one a day.

### How often should a small business post on social media?

Pick one or two networks where your customers are and post two or three times a week on each. A steady rhythm you can keep for months beats daily posting you abandon after a fortnight.

### Is posting every day too much on LinkedIn?

For most people, yes. LinkedIn posts keep getting views for two or three days, so daily posts mostly compete with each other. Two to five a week works better for nearly everyone.

### Does posting more often get more followers?

Usually, up to a point. More posts mean more chances to be seen, but reach per post falls as you post more. Consistency over months matters more than volume in any single week.

### What if I can't post consistently?

Batch it. Write a week of posts in one sitting and schedule them. Then you only need a few minutes a day for replies, which you can do from anywhere.

### Should I post the same thing on every network?

The same idea, yes. The same words, no. Each network has its own length limits and habits, so adjust the wording, and post at times that suit each network's audience.
