**To start a LinkedIn newsletter, click "Write article" from the post box, choose to create a newsletter, give it a name, description, publishing frequency and logo, then write and publish your first edition.** When it goes out, LinkedIn invites all your followers and connections to subscribe, which is what makes LinkedIn newsletters grow so fast. You need newsletter access first: LinkedIn looks for at least 150 followers or connections and a history of original posts.

Here's the full process, what to put in each field, and how to keep it going. Checked on 10 November 2026.

## Who can start a LinkedIn newsletter?

LinkedIn rolls newsletter access out gradually. Its help page for company Pages says Pages with **more than 150 followers or connections** and **recent original content** are eligible to be evaluated, and personal profiles are judged on similar signals. If you don't see the option:

- Grow past 150 followers or connections.
- Post original content (not just reposts) for a few weeks.
- Make sure your profile or Page is complete and follows LinkedIn's community policies.

Then check again; there's nothing to apply for.

## How to create a LinkedIn newsletter

1. On your LinkedIn home feed, click **Write article** (on a Page, open the Page's admin view and start an article from there).
2. In the article editor, open the publishing options and choose **Create a newsletter**.
3. Fill in the details:
   - **Title:** what it's about, in a few words. People subscribe to a clear promise ("Weekly B2B pricing teardowns"), not a clever name.
   - **Description:** one or two sentences on what readers get and how often.
   - **Frequency:** daily, weekly, biweekly or monthly. Pick one you can keep.
   - **Logo:** a square image, at least 300 × 300 pixels.
4. Click **Done**. Your newsletter now exists; it publishes nothing until you write an edition.
5. Write the **first edition** in the article editor: a headline, a cover image, and the body, with headings, bold, lists and links (articles have real formatting, unlike posts).
6. Click **Next**, write the post that will accompany it in the feed, and **Publish**.

## What happens when you publish the first edition

LinkedIn sends an **invitation to subscribe** to all your followers and connections when the first edition goes out. That's the biggest growth moment your newsletter will get, so make the first edition your best: a clear title, a strong opening paragraph and something genuinely useful.

After that:

- **Subscribers** get a notification, and usually an email, every time you publish an edition.
- **New followers and connections** are invited to subscribe.
- Each edition also appears in the feed as a post, like an article.

## How often should you publish?

Consistency beats frequency. Weekly is the most common rhythm and long enough to write something worth reading; monthly works for deep, research-heavy issues. Whatever you choose, publish on the same day each time, so subscribers learn when to expect you. Our [best time to post on LinkedIn](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin) guide suggests Tuesday to Thursday, late morning to mid-afternoon.

## Newsletter cover images and sizes

- **Edition cover image:** 1920 × 1080 pixels (16:9) works well for article covers.
- **Newsletter logo:** square, at least 300 × 300.
- **Images in the body:** 1080 pixels wide or more.

More sizes in our guide to [LinkedIn image sizes](/blog/linkedin-image-sizes).

## Turn each edition into a week of posts

A newsletter edition is a week of social posts waiting to happen. After you publish:

1. **Day 1:** the edition itself, with a short post saying why it matters this week.
2. **Days 2 to 4:** one post per key idea from the edition, each standing on its own, with a link to the edition in the first comment.
3. **On X and Bluesky:** short versions of the best line or number, linking back.
4. **Next week:** a "most-read last week" post before the new edition.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I create a newsletter on LinkedIn?

Click Write article, choose Create a newsletter, add a title, description, frequency and logo, then write and publish your first edition. You need newsletter access, which LinkedIn gives to accounts with at least 150 followers or connections and recent original posts.

### Why can't I create a newsletter on LinkedIn?

You probably don't have access yet. LinkedIn looks for more than 150 followers or connections and a history of original content, and rolls access out gradually. Keep posting and check again.

### Do my followers get notified about my LinkedIn newsletter?

Yes. When you publish the first edition, LinkedIn invites all your followers and connections to subscribe. Subscribers then get a notification, and usually an email, for each new edition.

### Is a LinkedIn newsletter free?

Yes. Creating and publishing a LinkedIn newsletter is free, and subscribing is free for readers.

### Can a company Page have a LinkedIn newsletter?

Yes. Pages with more than 150 followers and recent original content can be given access, and admins publish from the Page's admin view.

### How often should I publish a LinkedIn newsletter?

Weekly is the most common, and monthly works for longer issues. Pick a rhythm you can keep for months and publish on the same day each time.
