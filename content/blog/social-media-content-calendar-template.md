A content calendar is a plan of what you'll post, where and when. It sounds like admin, but it's the thing that turns "I should post more" into posts that actually go out. With one, you write in batches, spot gaps before they happen and stop staring at a blank composer at 9am.

This guide gives you a free template to copy, a simple system for filling it in an hour a week, and a sample week to start from.

## What a content calendar needs (and what it doesn't)

Most templates have too many columns. Here's what earns its place:

| Column | Why it's there |
| --- | --- |
| Date and time | When it goes out, in your audience's time zone |
| Network | Where it goes: X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Mastodon, TikTok, YouTube |
| Pillar | Which of your three to five themes it belongs to |
| Format | Text, image, thread, carousel, short video, long video |
| Hook | The first line, the bit that decides whether anyone reads on |
| Post | The text, or a link to the draft |
| Media | The image or video file, or what still needs making |
| Link | Where you want people to go, if anywhere |
| Status | Idea, drafted, ready, scheduled, published |
| Result | A note on how it did, filled in a week later |

You can skip "Result" at first. Don't skip "Status": it's how you see at a glance what still needs doing.

## Step 1: pick three to five content pillars

Pillars are the recurring themes you post about. They keep your feed coherent and make the weekly planning much faster, because you're filling slots rather than inventing from scratch. Good starting pillars:

- **Lessons**: what you've learned, mistakes, opinions.
- **Behind the scenes**: how things are made, what your week looks like.
- **Tips and how-tos**: useful, saveable advice.
- **Customer stories**: results, quotes, before and after.
- **News**: launches, updates, events. Keep this one small.

A healthy mix is mostly useful posts with a little promotion. If every post asks for something, people stop reading.

## Step 2: decide your rhythm per network

Write down how often you'll post on each network, and keep it modest. Two networks done well beats five done badly. Our guide to [how often to post](/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media) has a starting range for each network; for most people it's something like two or three LinkedIn posts a week and one X or Bluesky post a day.

Then turn that into fixed slots: LinkedIn on Monday and Thursday at 8:30, X every weekday at noon. Fixed slots are what make the calendar quick to fill. Our [best time to post tool](/tools/best-time-to-post) suggests good windows in your time zone.

## Step 3: fill a week in one sitting

Once a week, ideally the same day, spend an hour on this:

1. **Look at last week's results** and note what did well.
2. **Pick ideas from your Ideas tab** for each empty slot, mixing pillars.
3. **Write the hooks first.** If you can't write a good first line, the idea isn't ready.
4. **Write the posts**, starting with the longest version (usually LinkedIn) and cutting it down for X and Bluesky.
5. **List the media you need** and make it, or mark it for later.
6. **Schedule everything that's ready.**

## Step 4: adapt each post per network

The same idea works on several networks, but the same words rarely do. LinkedIn wants short paragraphs and a story; X wants it in 280 characters or a thread; Bluesky allows 300; Mastodon 500 with hashtags people actually use. Our guide to [cross-posting without it looking copy-pasted](/blog/cross-post-x-bluesky-mastodon-linkedin) covers each network's habits, and the [image sizes guide](/tools/social-media-image-sizes) covers the media.

## Step 5: schedule it and leave room for live posts

Fill about 80% of your slots in advance. Leave the rest for things that happen during the week: news, replies, a great customer message. A calendar that's 100% full makes you ignore good live moments.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a social media content calendar include?

At minimum: the date and time, the network, the post text, any media, and a status (idea, drafted, ready, scheduled, published). Content pillars and a results column make it more useful over time.

### Is there a free social media calendar template?

Yes. Copy the table in this guide into Google Sheets, Excel, Numbers or Notion. It has the columns most teams need and a sample week to edit.

### How far ahead should I plan social media content?

Plan themes a month ahead and write posts a week ahead. Leave about 20% of your slots open for things that happen during the week.

### How many content pillars should I have?

Three to five. Fewer feels repetitive, and more makes your feed feel unfocused and the planning slower.

### Should I use a spreadsheet or a scheduling tool?

A spreadsheet works for planning. A scheduling tool saves the copying and pasting, posts on time, and shows the whole week across networks on one calendar. Many people plan in a sheet and schedule in a tool.

### How long does it take to plan a week of social media?

About an hour for most small businesses and creators, once your pillars and slots are set. The first few weeks take longer.
